App concepts

Three app ideas I designed on my own. None has been tested with real users yet, and I say what I'd do next for each.

RideGambia

A ride-booking app for getting a taxi in a few taps. Four screens take someone from sign-up to choosing a driver.

Type
Self-initiated concept
Tool
Figma
Status
Not yet tested with riders

What I decided

  • Sign-up asks for your area, like Brikama, instead of a full address.
  • Phone numbers use the +220 format.
  • Fares are shown in dalasi.
  • Each driver card shows the car, rating, plate number and arrival time, so riders can check the driver before booking.
  • Driver contact details appear only after the ride is confirmed.

What's next

Replace the placeholder map with a real one, then watch five people book a ride and fix wherever they get stuck.

View in Figma

Nopal Nit

A mobile wallet for sending money in as few steps as possible. Three screens: home, send money, and confirmation.

Type
Self-initiated concept
Tool
Figma
Status
Not yet tested with users

What I decided

  • The home screen shows the balance first, with three actions under it: add money, send, and my cards.
  • Preset amounts sit above a number keypad, so a common amount takes one tap.
  • The confirmation screen reads like a receipt: transaction ID, date, recipient, amount, fees and total.

What's next

Test the send flow with five people and note where they hesitate.

View in Figma

SnackGo

An app for ordering snacks and ice cream in The Gambia. It's an early concept, and usability testing hasn't happened yet.

Type
Concept
Tool
Canva
Status
Early concept

What I decided

  • Category navigation, so people can jump between snacks and ice cream.
  • Product cards that are easy to scan.
  • Search and location cues at the top of the screen.
  • A cart screen that finishes the ordering flow.

What's next

Usability testing and further development.

How a project works

  1. Brief

    You tell me what the design is for, who will see it and where it will be used.

  2. Sketch

    I send two or three rough layout directions for you to react to.

  3. Refine

    You pick one, and I adjust it with your feedback until it's right.

  4. Deliver

    You get files sized for print and social media, ready to share.
